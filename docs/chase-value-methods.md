# Chase research methods and feasibility

## External research

- William Benter (1994) fits a fundamental race-level logit and combines it with the public's implied probabilities. A fixed market offset is a simpler nested test of incremental information. His reported experience concerns a different pari-mutuel market and period. [Original chapter](https://gwern.net/doc/statistics/decision/1994-benter.pdf)
- Lessmann, Sung and Johnson (2007) model relative performance and use conditional logit to turn it into a race-level probability book. Their rank-focused model selection motivates tracking winner identification alongside probability scores. An ordinal rating comparison is feasible here with much less complexity. [Original paper](https://www.ubplj.org/index.php/jpm/article/download/427/459)
- Spence et al. (2012) use in-race tracking to measure drafting and distance-dependent pacing. A prediction experiment would need historical, pre-race-available measurements of individual running styles and projected field interactions. Their measured in-race relationships do not establish a transferable Chase win model. [Original paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3391435/)

Its saved feature and capture timestamps support a historical experiment, with weaker vintage guarantees for upstream history than a new untouched capture. Cross-check the manifest and reject missing field snapshots, overlaps, ambiguous labels and outcomes unavailable at the fit/evaluation cutoff.

## Candidates and protocol

1. **Market-relative conditional logit with race-relative form ranks.** Build one Chase experiment. Hypothesis is that ordinal official rating and recent speed carry incremental information after pricing. Use two predictors, the within-field centred midranks of nonmissing official rating and best last-three jump speed, divided by the number of observed field values minus one. Missing or singleton values map to the midpoint. Fit both a standalone softmax and a version with log market probability as a fixed offset. Compare both against the same market and Chase V2. Tissue uses these raw inputs among many others. This test uses the within-field order, discards the magnitude of rating gaps and learns a separate market-relative likelihood. No Tissue probability, rank or score enters either fit.
2. **Disagreement as a diagnostic first.** Compare T1/T2/T3 and the market favourite, including the gap between independent rating/speed positions and market position. This can distinguish whether T3 tends to occupy an ordinary mid-market price band or identifies independently well-rated horses priced lower by the market. A second fit using gaps would overlap heavily with the first candidate. Revisit only after a separately specified test, including a control for market calibration.
3. **Form and race-condition suitability.** A future model could combine comparable-condition runs, recent improvement and projected field pace. The frozen books support simple context descriptions today. A defensible pace interaction or condition-specific model needs an audited as-of record of prior comparable runs and consistent style measurements. Deferred.

Fit on eligible Chase development races scheduled 20 October–4 November 2026 using settlements available before 5 November. Evaluate separately on eligible 5–18 November generation races. Use only complete captured market books and complete settled fields with valid winner credit, requiring distinct races and runners. The fixed penalty is 0.05, applied as half the squared coefficient norm times that value to mean training log loss. There is no hyperparameter search or ROI-based selection. The validation dates have already informed Chase rank research, so all results from that period remain exploratory.

Fixed comparisons use race-mean multiclass log loss and summed multiclass Brier score, top-ranked winner count (stable runner ID breaks ties), calibration by fixed probability bins, and winner credit versus market expectation. Price bands use the existing badge bands. Top choice per book is fixed for monetary comparisons. Use common canonical-priced races for the monetary comparison and report coverage before P/L. Report paired race loss differences with a date-cluster interval and chronological slices. Only 2026 Chase is in this experiment.
