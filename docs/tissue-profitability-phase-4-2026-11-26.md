# Chase profitability development

**Phase 4 · 26 November 2026**

**Recommend one future shadow experiment: use frozen T3 in confirmed Chase handicaps with recorded Class 4, 5 or 6, and retain frozen T2 everywhere else.** The switch improved matched final-SP P/L by **£39.76 on 753 races in 2025** and **£76.99 on 557 in earlier 2026**. It also beat T2 at verified bookmaker prices by **£10.29 on 52 earlier races** and **£8.42 on 46 November races**. The historical policy still lost money, and the November captured gain falls to **£0.42** when the most favourable day is omitted. This is a bounded hypothesis worth testing on new races.

## Why Chase T3 looks better at SP

Each row uses identical races and £1 settled win stakes for both ranks. Phase 4 matches T2 and T3 directly. Phase 3 also required T1 to settle, which explains the different November counts. The November source ranks and bookmaker quotes were frozen before off. Earlier 2026 bookmaker prices come from timestamped pre-off cards with verified roster matches, attached to retrospective ranks. Final SP is always a separate settlement and market basis. No 2025 captured market was available for this comparison.

**Winning payouts drove the historical advantage.** T3 had 24 fewer winners in 2025 and 21 fewer in earlier 2026, with the same number of bets. Using T2's average gross payout, this cost £115.67 and £94.87 respectively. Larger average T3 winning payouts added £140.32 and £144.85. The extra return was wider than the longest-price tier. Across all handicaps at decimal SP 11.00 or more, T3 backed 300 in 2025 and 206 in earlier 2026, returning −£70 and −£24. T2 returned +£6 and +£18 on its own bets at those prices. All those bets are included in the headline. Harjo at 26.00 was the largest 2025 T3 handicap winner. Removing that race reverses the broad 2025 edge to −£1.35.

**The quoted subset explains most of the price-basis discrepancy.** Earlier 2026 had 84 quoted pairs out of 960. T3 trailed by £19.21 at capture and still trailed by £13.08 at SP on those exact races. Movement improved T3's relative return by £6.13, but the other 876 races contributed **+£63.07 at SP** and lack corresponding pre-off quotes. On the quoted subset, six fewer T3 wins cost £24.90 at T2's average final payout, outweighing £11.82 from richer T3 winning prices. Nine of 12 T3 winners drifted from the archived quote to SP. The comparison is consistent with quote coverage selecting a different race mix. It cannot establish what the missing quotes would have paid.

**November's quoted return came from payouts with equal winner counts.** On the 83 pairs, both ranks won 18 times and T3's average winning odds were longer at both prices. Captured T3 put 43 selections in market ranks 1–3, versus 56 for T2. T3 gained £6.26 when its captured price was shorter than T2's and £3.29 otherwise, while both groups lost ground to T2 in the earlier quoted cohort. Final-SP ordering also changed direction across years. A rule based on market preference alone lacks stable support. - **Distance.** Intermediate chases favoured T2 in all three SP periods. Short chases were the strongest November pocket, but the earlier 2026 short-distance gain disappears on the common Jan–14 October window (**−£4.73**). Staying-handicap T1 remains a useful contrast. It gained £75.21 and £88.29 historically against T2 before losing £10.75 at November SP and £11.42 at November captured prices in Phase 3.
- **Going.** Good or faster favoured T2 historically and reversed in November. Good to Soft / Yielding favoured T3 in the historical SP replays by £106.14 and £38.50, then reversed to −£18.45 in November. Heavy had positive T3 results in both earlier periods but no matched November or earlier quoted Heavy cohort. Reported result going is diagnostic. A pre-off going rule would need the original timestamped description.
- **Field size and course.** Fields of 7–10 favoured T3 at SP in all three periods, but lost £22.84 against T2 on 31 earlier quoted pairs. Historical sizes use the realised starter field and November sizes use the frozen card. Hereford's handicap T3 gap was +£30.49 on 30 and +£25.88 on 21 historical races, then −£3.50 on one November race. Course and size effects may reflect the card mix. Neither justifies a separate gate.
- **Class.** Class 4–6 is the only tested policy trigger with positive relative cash P/L in all three SP periods and both genuine bookmaker samples. Keep a separate unknown-class group. A course, country or race title is insufficient evidence to assign a numeric class. On the matched T2/T3 data, missing or other class accounts for 156, 130 and 11 handicap pairs across the three periods.
- **Form and score.** In paired handicaps T3 carried a lower measured OR by 1.15, 0.62 and 0.97 points on average, and lower measured latest Jump Speed by 2.43, 2.22 and 0.68. When T3 had the stronger latest measured Speed, SP gains were **+£10.02, −£29.37 and +£13.30**. Earlier verified quotes on 26 such pairs showed **−£21.50**. Best Last 3 Speed and the historical weight-adjusted performance rating also gave unstable advantages. In earlier 2026, stronger T3 Best Last 3 Speed returned £57.25 less than T2 on 427 SP pairs. These are stored prior-run Jump metrics, which can include hurdles. The evidence lacks comparable Chase-only Speed, exact recent finishing form and a frozen weight-adjusted performance field across all three periods. The appendix also compares historical prior win rate and recency. Mean T2 minus T3 log-probability gaps were 0.181, 0.193 and 0.154. November T3's realised 20 wins versus 14.10 model-expected exceeds the earlier 2026 result of 150 versus 151.24. There is no stable broad rank-calibration or faster-horse explanation.

## Three fixed policies

Each starts from original T2 and switches to **unchanged frozen T3** only when the pre-off rule is true and a unique T3 exists. Otherwise it retains T2. There is no abstention or fallback after a post-decision withdrawal. Price is used for evaluation, not selection.

The appendix gives unchanged T2 and whole-policy P/L, stakes, ROI, winners gained and missed, and sensitivities. C gained despite sacrificing seven winners in 2025 and one in earlier 2026. Its winners paid 5.15 versus 4.64 and 5.51 versus 4.69 respectively. On the same 52 earlier quoted races, both ranks had ten winners and C gained +£14.88 at SP as well as +£10.29 captured. No T3 in that subset won at 11.00 or greater. At captured prices on the 46 November switches, T3 had 12 winners to T2's 11 and also paid slightly more per win.

C is supported by **1,310 matched historical switch opportunities**, about **45% of the 2,888 matched historical Chase races**. On all 140 settled November opportunities, including refunds, C switched 55 races (**39%**) and returned **+£18.47 at SP versus −£4.95 for T2**, with 33 versus 28 winners. On the 125 complete-board opportunities it switched 51 races and returned **+£17.51 captured versus +£1.59**, with 29 versus 26 winners. Unrounded P/L is used for differences.

## Validation requirements and decision

C's historical SP lead survives both the largest T3 winner and the most favourable day in each year. In the common January–14 October window it improved P/L by **£86.75 on 537 switches in 2025** and **£55.68 on 526 in 2026**. Omitting the strongest course still leaves **£13.27 and £31.76** respectively. On 52 earlier verified-quote triggers the day/winner sensitivity reduces the captured lead to **£2.79**. On 46 November quoted triggers it reduces the captured lead to **£0.42**, and the same-race SP advantage becomes **−£3.20** when the strongest winner's race is removed. The historic whole policy remained negative at SP, at −£89.96 and −£115.31 across 1,677 and 1,211 opportunities.

The 2025 local reconstruction covers the Chase base-model development year. Earlier 2026 uses the stored Shadow promotion predictions that informed frozen Chase V2 selection. Both historical series condition on the realised starter rosters. The narrower class rule emerged from these known data and November was already examined. Verified earlier quote triggers are limited to September–October. No period here is untouched validation. Historical and frozen forecasts retain their original provenance and the study never substitutes SP for a missing captured quote. **Test C alone on genuinely new race IDs.** Freeze the Chase family, confirmed handicap status, numeric class, original ranks and both timestamped prices from the exact same book. Keep unknown class and missing quotes visible. Record no replacement on NR or void, preserve all winners, and report £1 P/L per eligible opportunity and ROI per settled stake separately at both bases. A possible fixed first review is 100 eligible switches across at least 30 racing dates, with matched captured quotes as the primary monetary endpoint and SP as corroboration. Treat negative captured incremental P/L, an SP-only gain, or a gain that depends on one day with losses elsewhere as evidence against the hypothesis. Keep the rule fixed through the review.
